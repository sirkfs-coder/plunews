Why Even “Official” Lists Can Steer You Wrong

Independent leagues like the Frontier League are full of surprises, teams move stadiums or rebrand with little warning. Relying on last year’s list (or even the one on the league website) can send you to an empty lot or a ballpark with a different name on the sign.

Illustrative example: My first time chasing a game in Joliet, I typed “Route 66 Stadium” into my GPS. When I got there, every sign read “Duly Health and Care Field.” The game was still happening (thankfully), but for someone going off an old list, or aiming for a different event, that kind of change could have turned into a wasted drive.

How Fans Avoid Ballpark Blunders

If you want to actually see games where and when they happen, not just collect a list, you’ll need some habits that protect you from outdated info:

  • Double-check both league and team websites: Teams often update their own sites faster than the league does. If anything looks inconsistent (stadium name or location), trust the team’s site or contact them directly.
  • Look for city AND state/province: Some teams share similar names or play in regions that cross borders. For example: Quebec Capitales play in Quebec City (QC), not some other town named Quebec.
  • Scan for recent press releases or news: Naming rights can change overnight. If your map app or ticket says something unfamiliar (“Clover Stadium” instead of “Palisades Credit Union Park”), check for an announcement online.
  • Ask before you go: A quick call or email to the team’s ticket office can clear up confusion in seconds.

Common Pitfalls, and How to Dodge Them

  • Stadium names changing due to sponsorships: This happens almost every season somewhere in the league.
  • Teams quietly moving: It doesn’t happen often, but when it does, old schedules and maps are nearly useless.
  • Outdated online lists: Even Wikipedia sometimes lags behind reality.

Composite example: A group of friends planned a road trip around what they thought was the Boulders’ stadium, only to get thrown off by new signage at “Clover Stadium.” They made it because another fan posted about it online that morning.

What Works Best: Simple Habits That Save You Headaches

  1. Before you leave home (or publish anything), visit your team’s official site and scan their schedule page for stadium info.
  2. If anything looks odd, like a new stadium name, search “[Team Name] press release” or check their latest social media posts.
  3. Keep a simple spreadsheet with team names, stadiums, cities/provinces, plus the date you last checked them. This is especially useful if you’re planning multiple trips or helping others.
  4. When in doubt, email or call the ticket office, they’re usually happy to confirm details.

Encouragement for First-Timers

It can feel like extra work to double-check everything, but most fans who take these small steps avoid wasted trips and get better stories out of their visits. Expect change; treat each trip as a chance to discover something new about independent baseball’s wild side.

The best next step? Pick any team above and verify their stadium info right now using their website or contact form. It only takes two minutes, and means you’ll arrive at the right place with confidence this summer.

Enjoy your ballpark adventures!
