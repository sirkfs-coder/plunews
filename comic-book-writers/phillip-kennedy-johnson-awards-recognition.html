If you’ve ever tried to nail down exactly which awards a comics creator has won versus just been nominated for, you know it’s surprisingly tricky. With Phillip Kennedy Johnson, the confusion is real: fan sites, publisher blurbs, and wiki entries sometimes blur the lines between nominations and wins, or credit team wins as solo achievements. If you want a clean, accurate list for a profile or professional materials, here’s how to do it, without falling into the usual traps.

Quick Reference: Johnson’s Major Awards and Nominations (as of June 2024)

  • 2022 Eisner Award
    Best Limited Series, Winner (for “The Last God,” with the creative team)

  • Ringo Award
    Best Writer, Nominee (multiple years; not a winner as of June 2024)

  • Harvey Award
    Best Writer, Nominee (not a winner as of June 2024)

If you see anyone claiming more than this, or solo wins for these categories, pause and double check.

Why Award Lists Are So Often Wrong

It’s tempting to trust lists from big fan wikis or publisher press releases. But comics awards are confusing by nature:

  • Many awards go to teams, not just one writer.
  • Nominations get repeated as wins in third-hand summaries.
  • Year-old info lingers online after new results come out.

Illustrative example: I once prepped an event bio for a different comics creator and almost listed him as an Eisner winner. Two popular fan sites said he’d won, but only when I checked the official Eisner archive did I spot he’d merely been nominated that year. That could have been embarrassing for both of us if left uncorrected.

The Step-by-Step Process That Never Fails

Here’s my own checklist for confirming Johnson’s honors before putting them in writing:

  1. Go straight to the source:
  • Use official award websites: Eisner, Ringo, Harvey
  • Search not just by name, but by project title (like “The Last God” or “Superman: Action Comics”).
  1. Be strict about "win" vs "nomination":
  • Clearly label each entry as “Winner” or “Nominee” with the year.
  • Don’t conflate being on a shortlist with taking home the trophy.
  1. Account for teamwork:
  • If a project won Best Limited Series but included several creators, say so.
  • Credit Johnson specifically as writer within that context.
  1. Keep informal recognition in its own lane:
  • Positive reviews, “Best Of” lists, convention invites, they matter, but should be listed separately from major awards.
  1. Re-check right before publishing:
  • Most big awards announce results in early summer.
  • If your article or profile will run after June, do one last pass through award sites since late-breaking updates happen often.

What Counts as Meaningful Recognition?

Formal awards like the Eisners are industry gold. A win there, especially for something like Best Limited Series, is top-tier validation, Johnson earned this with his team for "The Last God." Ringo and Harvey nominations show his peers recognize his talent; even without wins yet, consistent nominations build real credibility.

But don’t overlook editorial trust. When DC hands someone flagship series like “Superman” or “Alien,” that’s a silent endorsement: publishers put their biggest properties in safe hands only if they’re confident in the creator’s vision and reliability. This kind of trust might not win trophies but says plenty about standing inside the industry.

How to Present Johnson’s Achievements Accurately

When you’re ready to write your piece or update your records:

  • Start with what can be proven:
    “Phillip Kennedy Johnson won the 2022 Eisner Award for Best Limited Series (‘The Last God,’ with his creative team). He has also received multiple Ringo and Harvey Award nominations.”

  • Add context if needed:
    “As of June 2024, these are his highest-profile recognitions; always refer back to official sources or publisher announcements for any late updates.”

  • List informal honors separately:
    For example: “Featured on multiple ‘Best Comics of the Year’ lists; frequent guest at major conventions.”

What To Do With Unverifiable Claims

Sometimes you’ll encounter mentions of smaller festival awards or international honors that aren’t easily checked online. Unless you can find confirmation from:

  • An official winners’ list,
  • A publisher press release,
  • Or Johnson’s own site/socials,

…it’s best to leave those out until you can verify them solidly.

Final Encouragement, and Your Next Steps

Don’t let fear of missing something keep you from presenting Johnson’s achievements with pride! As long as you rely on primary sources and clearly state whether it was a win or nomination (and don’t overstate solo credit), your profile will stand out for its accuracy, a rare thing in comics circles where errors spread fast.

Before hitting send or print:

  1. Double-check official award archives for last-minute changes.
  2. Clearly separate major awards from honorable mentions.
  3. Give yourself credit, you’re doing more due diligence than most!

If stuck on a particular claim? Shoot an email to Johnson’s agent/editor or reach out via his professional channels; most creators are happy to clarify what they’ve actually won.

You’ll be helping set a higher standard, not just for Phillip Kennedy Johnson profiles but for comics journalism overall. And who knows? Maybe someone will look up YOUR work next time they need to get things right.
