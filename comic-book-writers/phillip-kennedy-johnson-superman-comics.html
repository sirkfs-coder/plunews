If you haven’t read Action Comics since before Phillip Kennedy Johnson took over, you might be surprised by how different Superman feels these days. I’ve followed Superman through countless creative teams, but few have shaken things up like Johnson. His run doesn’t just move Superman out of Metropolis, it throws him into the deep end, raising tough questions about what it means to be a hero when you’re far from home and not at your strongest.

Here’s what really stands out about this era, where it might surprise (or frustrate) you, and how to get the most out of it as a reader.

Are You Ready for a Story That Demands Commitment?

Johnson writes in long arcs with little hand-holding. Each issue builds directly on the last, and there aren’t many convenient recaps or pauses for new readers. For longtime fans who are tired of stories resetting every few months, this approach is refreshing, actions have consequences, trauma lingers, and growth sticks around.

But if you’re hoping to jump in anywhere or grab a quick one-off adventure, you may feel lost. For example, if you haven’t read Brian Michael Bendis’s run (where Jon Kent ages up quickly and Superman reveals his identity), some context will be missing. I remember spending an evening flipping back through old issues and double-checking character histories online just to keep up.

What This Means:

  • If you love deep continuity and don’t mind piecing together details as you go, Johnson rewards your investment.
  • If you want something self-contained or easy to follow on a tight schedule, expect some initial confusion.

Trading Metropolis for Warworld: A New Kind of Superman Story

Instead of daily life at the Daily Planet or classic showdowns in Metropolis, most of Johnson’s run takes place off-world on Warworld, a hostile alien planet ruled by Mongul. The visuals are gritty and immersive thanks to artists like Daniel Sampere and Riccardo Federici. But this setting means less Lois Lane, Jimmy Olsen, or familiar villains. At one point midway through the “Warworld Saga,” I realized it had been several issues since Lois even appeared; I missed her presence almost as much as Clark did.

Upside:
You see Superman pushed in ways he rarely is on Earth, physically exhausted, morally tested by impossible choices, leading a group of oppressed aliens rather than his usual friends.

Downside:
Much of what makes Superman “Clark Kent” gets pushed aside: no newsroom banter, no Smallville flashbacks, no classic rogues gallery. If that’s your comfort zone for Superman stories, this run can feel like a departure.

Superman Without Full Powers: Do You Prefer Vulnerability or Spectacle?

One of the boldest choices here is making Superman weaker than usual. He bleeds. He tires out. Sometimes he loses, and not just in alternate universes or imaginary stories. This vulnerability raises the stakes for every fight and makes his leadership more meaningful because he can’t rely solely on brute strength.

A composite example from my reading group: One fan was excited to see Superman genuinely struggle and inspire hope despite his limits; another felt let down because they missed seeing him effortlessly juggle spaceships while giving inspiring speeches. For them, “Superman in chains” felt less heroic than the old-school powerhouse.

How to Decide:

  • If your favorite thing about Superman is how he inspires others even when he’s vulnerable, or if you enjoy seeing him confront situations where courage matters more than strength, this era works beautifully.
  • If you read Superman for big feats and classic optimism (“faster than a speeding bullet!”), these issues might leave you wishing for earlier eras.

The Pacing: Slow Burn With Big Payoff (If You Stick With It)

Johnson writes with collected editions in mind, major plotlines unfold over 6+ issues before resolution. On one hand, this gives us deeper characters (Mongul becomes more than just a muscle-bound villain) and lets Warworld feel like a real place instead of set dressing. On the other hand, individual issues can seem slow or heavy on setup.

I almost dropped the series around Action Comics #1036 because it felt like nothing was moving forward, then hit #1038 and suddenly all those pieces snapped together in ways that made me appreciate the groundwork laid earlier.

Advice From Experience:
If you try this run, commit to reading at least Action Comics #1030–1035 (“Warworld Rising”) before deciding if it’s for you. The early setup pays off later, and judging too soon risks missing what makes this story unique.

Where to Start (and What Not to Miss)

Getting started is straightforward if you know where to look:

  • Start with Action Comics #1030 or grab the “Warworld Rising” trade paperback.
  • Don’t skip Action Comics Annual 2021, it gives crucial background on Mongul that makes later chapters hit harder.
  • Check out DC Universe Infinite (or your library app) if you want affordable access.
  • Optional bonus: Tom Taylor’s Superman: Son of Kal-El runs alongside for Jon Kent context, but isn’t required.
  • Expect more violence and darker themes than typical Superman runs; preview an issue first if that’s a concern.

Is This Your Kind of Superman Story Right Now?

The biggest question isn’t whether Johnson’s run is good, it’s whether it matches what you’re looking for. There are easier entry points if you want nostalgia or lighthearted adventures. But if you’re open to something challenging, a story about leadership under pressure and hope surviving against impossible odds, this era delivers something special.

If that sounds exciting rather than intimidating, here’s what I recommend:
Pick up Action Comics #1030 through at least #1035 (digitally or physically). Give yourself time with these first six issues before making any judgments; don’t worry if some details are unfamiliar at first. Watch how Clark inspires others even when his own powers falter, that core idea is where Johnson’s vision shines brightest.

And if you find yourself missing Lois Lane or classic Metropolis scenes along the way? You’re not alone, I did too, but sometimes stepping outside your comfort zone with Superman leads to stories that stick with you long after they end.
