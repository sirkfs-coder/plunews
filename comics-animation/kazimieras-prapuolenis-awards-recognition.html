If you’ve ever tried to document the achievements of a creative professional, you know how confusing it can get. Cartoonists and animation writers like Kazimieras Prapuolenis (“Kaz”) often rack up impressive honors, but the details are scattered across old websites, show credits, and awards lists. Here’s how to make sense of it, and help others quickly understand why Kaz’s recognitions actually matter.

1. Stick to Major, Verifiable Honors First

The best place to start is with awards that have public records, nothing beats an official list.

A. National Cartoonists Society Award (1997)
Kaz won the National Cartoonists Society’s Magazine and Book Illustration Award in 1997. This is not just another trophy: it’s one of the highest peer-voted honors in American cartooning. The NCS keeps a complete archive of award winners on their website, so this recognition is rock-solid.

B. Emmy Nominations (Team Credits)
Here’s where things get tricky. Animation Emmys are usually awarded to the show itself, not every person who worked on it. Kaz was a credited writer and storyboard artist during Emmy-nominated seasons of “SpongeBob SquarePants” and on “Camp Lazlo” during its nomination year. You’ll find his name in episode credits, cross-check these with the official Emmy nominations for those seasons (IMDb helps here).

Composite Example:
I once helped a colleague write a bio for someone who worked on an animated series that won an Emmy. We made the mistake of saying she “won an Emmy,” when in fact only the show did, the individual wasn’t listed as a recipient. We had to rewrite that line with care: “writer on the Emmy-winning series X.” Small difference, but it matters for accuracy.

2. Don’t List Unverified or Vague Mentions

It’s tempting to include every festival appearance or minor shortlisting, but unless you can back it up with an official program or reputable source, skip it. Listing unconfirmed recognitions only makes your summary weaker.

If you want to mention other distinctions, like being featured at comics festivals, just state them plainly:

  • Example: “His work has been selected for exhibition at independent comics festivals including [event], reflecting ongoing respect within the indie comics community.”

3. Always Add Context: Why Does Each Honor Matter?

Don’t just stack up award names; give readers a reason to care.

  • The NCS Award is chosen by top industry peers, it signals genuine respect from fellow cartoonists.
  • Being part of an Emmy-nominated team means Kaz contributed creatively to shows recognized as among the best in TV animation.

Someone unfamiliar with comics or animation should be able to read your list and instantly see why these recognitions stand out.

4. How To List Kaz’s Key Awards: A Simple Template

Here’s a clear way to present his most important recognitions:

Kazimieras Prapuolenis (“Kaz”) – Selected Awards & Distinctions

  • National Cartoonists Society Magazine and Book Illustration Award (1997): One of American cartooning’s top peer-selected honors.
  • Writer/storyboard artist on Emmy-nominated seasons: Credited for work on “SpongeBob SquarePants” and “Camp Lazlo” during years those shows received Emmy nominations (see IMDb for details).
  • Featured exhibitor at alternative comics festivals: His art has been included at respected indie events (if you have sources).

Sources for verification:

  • NCS Awards Archive
  • Kaz’s IMDb page

5. What To Do When Details Are Missing

Don’t guess dates or overstate involvement if you can’t confirm specifics. For team awards like Emmys, always clarify Kaz’s exact credited role instead of implying he personally took home the trophy.

Your Next Steps: How To Get It Right Every Time

  1. Double-check each honor using official sources (NCS archives, IMDb credits, award websites).
  2. Write a one-sentence explanation after each major recognition, why does this award matter?
  3. For team awards, clearly state Kaz’s role during relevant years.
  4. If in doubt about an accolade, leave it out until you can verify.
  5. Before publishing or sending your list anywhere, run through this checklist again.

Taking these steps will save you from awkward corrections later, and ensures Kaz gets credit for what he truly accomplished.

When I look back at bios I’ve written (and fixed), being careful with honors isn’t just about accuracy, it’s about respect for the artist and their field. With these tips, you’ll present Kazimieras Prapuolenis’s achievements clearly, honestly, and with real authority.

If you’re stuck verifying something specific about Kaz’s career that isn’t covered here, try reaching out directly to event organizers or checking archived interviews, they sometimes contain those missing details no database has yet caught up with.

And if you discover something new? Update your list right away! Accurate recognition helps preserve both history and reputation, two things every creative deserves.
