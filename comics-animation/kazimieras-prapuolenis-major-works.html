Tracking the full range of Kazimieras Prapuolenis’s (Kaz’s) comics and animation work is more like detective work than a straightforward search. Below, I’ll walk you through the main categories of his output, common pitfalls, and proven steps to actually locate what you’re after. This isn’t just a list, it’s a set of real-world tactics, with specifics you can use right away.

Why “Collected Editions” Leave Gaps

Many people assume that picking up Fantagraphics’ Underworld collections will cover most of Kaz’s comics work. In reality, these books are selective, no single series compiles every strip he published.

Key Titles:

  • Underworld: From Hoboken to Hollywood
  • Two Stories Tall
  • Sidetrack City

Each volume covers different years and strips. Because “Underworld” ran in many alternative newspapers (alt-weeklies), some strips appeared only in certain cities or issues. Editorial choices, lost archives, or rights problems mean some material never made it into any book.

Composite Example:
A researcher looking for every “Underworld” strip from 2001 discovered that nearly a year’s worth had run only in the Philadelphia City Paper. That run wasn’t in any Fantagraphics collection or online archive, and some issues were missing from library microfilm. In cases like this, even the most careful library search leaves holes.

What To Do:

  • Treat Fantagraphics collections as partial sources.
  • If completeness matters (for academic projects or curation), check which years each book covers.
  • For missing strips, try:
  • Contacting libraries with print archives of local alt-weeklies (especially in cities where Kaz was published).
  • Posting requests on comics collector forums, sometimes fans will share scans of rare material.
  • Asking Fantagraphics directly for coverage details if you’re unsure.

Anthology Stories: RAW Magazine, The New Yorker, SpongeBob Comics

Kaz has contributed to a range of anthologies and magazines, each with its own quirks.

RAW Magazine:
His stories appeared under different name variations (“Kaz,” “Kazimieras P.,” etc.) and aren’t always indexed consistently. RAW changed format over time; not all issues are equally well documented.

The New Yorker:
Some cartoons are archived under “Kaz,” others under his full name. The magazine rarely indexes by pseudonym.

SpongeBob Comics:
Contributions include stories, art, and sometimes editorial roles. Credits are often inside the front cover; online lists may skip over non-main features or editorial work.

Illustrative Example:
A student writing about experimental comics missed two of Kaz’s RAW stories because they searched only his full name in magazine databases. Only after asking around on collector forums did they learn about the alternate credits.

How to Find These Works:

  • For RAW: Look at scans or physical copies, don’t trust generic indexes.
  • For The New Yorker: Search both “Kaz” and “Kazimieras Prapuolenis.”
  • For SpongeBob Comics: Get images of the issue indices or consult fan-maintained spreadsheets for precise credits.

If you’re building an authoritative bibliography, always double-check using multiple sources rather than relying on publisher catalogs alone.

Standalone Comic Books: Spotting Overlap Before You Buy

Some standalone titles by Kaz (Buzzbomb, The Underworld Treasury, etc.) contain original material; others reprint strips from earlier collections or anthologies. This overlap can be frustrating if you’re hoping for new content, or need non-redundant sources for research.

How To Check:

  • Compare table-of-contents pages whenever possible.
  • Read publisher blurbs carefully (some will note if content is newly collected or previously published).
  • Search comics discussion boards, collectors often flag duplicated content early on.

If you’re collecting on a budget or want unique material, prioritize volumes known for original stories (Buzzbomb is one example).

Animation Work: Confusing Credits

Kaz has worked as a storyboard artist and writer on several animated series, including SpongeBob SquarePants. Credit listings vary widely:

  • Sometimes he’s listed as “Kaz,” sometimes as “Kaz Prapuolenis.”
  • Some episode guides omit storyboarders entirely.
  • Studio records are often inconsistent.

How To Verify His Role:

  • Look at actual episode credits (screen captures or DVD menus).
  • Check production blogs for detailed breakdowns per episode.
  • IMDb can help but isn’t always complete; official show websites are more reliable when available.

Strategies That Actually Help You Find Missing Material

Here are concrete steps with high success rates:

  1. Interlibrary Loan: Use your local library to request anthologies or newspaper runs from other institutions.
  2. Collector Forums: Ask specific questions on Reddit’s r/comicbooks, Facebook groups focused on alt-comics, or specialized message boards, members often have rare scans or leads.
  3. Publisher Inquiries: Fantagraphics staff usually respond to direct questions about their collected editions’ coverage.
  4. Try Name Variations: Always search using “Kaz,” “Kazimieras Prapuolenis,” and other permutations, especially for anthology appearances.
  5. Academic Databases: Although less thorough for comics, JSTOR and ProQuest sometimes index journalism studies articles referencing alt-comics publications, worth checking for citations that might point you to obscure venues.

One High-Payoff Step to Start With

If your goal is either thorough citation or building a serious collection, make yourself a simple spreadsheet listing every venue where Kaz has been published:

  • Alt-weeklies
  • Anthologies
  • Standalone books
    Track which works you already have (“collected”), which are missing (“uncollected”), and which need further confirmation (“uncertain”). This overview makes it much easier to spot gaps before you waste time chasing down duplicate material, or miss rare pieces entirely.

I’ve found that investing just an hour into this mapping exercise upfront saves much more time later, and turns what can feel like chaos into a manageable project.

Chasing down Kaz’s full creative output takes patience, but it’s rewarding if you enjoy uncovering hidden corners of comics history. If you hit dead ends or confusing overlaps along the way, don’t get discouraged; most collectors run into these same snags at first. Take it one step at a time, ask questions in friendly communities, and celebrate each piece you track down, the underground trail is worth following all the way through.
