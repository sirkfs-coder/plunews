Major Events and Annual Shows of Empire Wrestling Federation: How Fans Actually work through the EWF Calendar

If you’re trying to figure out which Empire Wrestling Federation shows are truly can’t-miss, you’re not alone. When I first got into EWF, I found myself scanning forums and showing up way too late for big events, always wondering how everyone else seemed to know exactly when to buy tickets or which dates were worth traveling for. Over time, and after a few hard lessons, I picked up on what really matters in the EWF fan world.

Let me break down the real annual landmarks, what actually happens around them, and how you can set yourself up for a better experience next time.

Two Pillars: The Anniversary Extravaganza and Jingle Slam

Talk to any longtime EWF attendee and these two dates come up every year:

  • Anniversary Extravaganza (usually May): This is the centerpiece. People drive in from all over Southern California (and sometimes farther). The parking lot’s full early, lines form before doors open, and there’s a buzz that doesn’t happen at regular shows. There’s usually an exclusive event t-shirt at the merch table, and you’ll see fans comparing old programs or taking group photos with friends they only see once a year. Most years, they end with something big, like a steel cage match or a special alumni main event.
  • Jingle Slam (usually December): This show has its own energy. It’s packed with families bringing toys for the holiday drive; some kids even dress up as their favorite wrestlers or wear Santa hats. Past years have included things like a Santa Battle Royal or “Santa’s Helpers” tag matches. It feels more community-driven, afterwards, people stick around for photos, autographs, and handing out treats.

Illustrative example: The first year I went to Jingle Slam, I saw an entire row of fans each holding wrapped gifts for the toy drive, not because anyone told them to, but because it was just “what you do.” That sense of shared tradition is what keeps people coming back.

Are there other recurring events? Occasionally EWF will run special tournaments or themed nights (like summer “Battle Bowl” cards), but attendance and hype almost always peak at these two annual shows.

How Fans Get Dates First, and Why Waiting Is Risky

EWF does not operate on a fixed national circuit. Dates sometimes shift based on venue conflicts or local events. Here’s what actually works:

  • Set Reminders: Most regulars put calendar alerts eight weeks ahead of May and December to check EWF’s website and main social feeds (Instagram is usually fastest with updates; Facebook gets full event listings).
  • Ask Directly: After missing my first presale by accident one year, I started messaging staff through Instagram DM whenever I had travel plans. They were friendly about confirming tentative dates, even letting me know when alumni might appear.
  • Watch Ticket Drops: If front row seats disappear within hours of release online (often via Brown Paper Tickets), expect a packed house. For these marquee shows, door tickets are rarely available by showtime.
  • Double-check Before Heading Out: Date changes happen, sometimes last-minute due to venue rules or emergencies. If you’re traveling far or need media access, confirm everything 48 hours before.

Traditions That Stick, And Little Surprises That Keep Things Fresh

Why do these events build such loyalty? It comes down to comfort mixed with just enough novelty:

  • Anniversary shows nearly always deliver a classic match type (steel cage or battle royal), but who headlines is kept under wraps until close to show day.
  • Jingle Slam mixes in new holiday-themed elements each year, one time candy canes at intermission, another time gift raffles, with returning traditions like the toy drive.
  • Small traditions matter: For example, there’s often the same churro cart outside Covina shows in May; fans bring custom signs for favorite returning wrestlers; some volunteer at merch tables just for these annual cards.

These details make even casual attendees feel invested, the kind of thing that turns “I’ll go if I’m free” into “We can’t miss this.”

Common Mistakes First-Timers Make, and How To Avoid Them

Composite example: A friend once planned his whole December around Jingle Slam assuming he could grab tickets at the door like usual. He arrived to find it sold out, and spent half an hour peering through a side window instead of seeing the matches up close.

Here are pitfalls to watch for:

  • Assuming Walk-Up Tickets Will Be Available: Both anniversary and holiday shows often sell out ahead of time.
  • Relying Only on Social Media Hype: Rumors about surprise appearances circulate every year; only announcements from official accounts or direct messages are reliable.
  • Ignoring Possible Card Changes: Independent wrestling means flexibility is key; injuries can force last-minute swaps.
  • Underestimating Traffic: Especially if you’re coming from outside Covina, leave extra time unless you want to miss opening bell.

Getting More Than Just A Seat: Becoming Part of the Tradition

Long-term fans don’t just show up, they plan carefully:

  1. Mark your calendar now for May (Anniversary) and December (Jingle Slam).
  2. Two months out: Check EWF’s site and Instagram every week; set phone alerts if needed.
  3. If you’re unsure about anything, especially if traveling, send a polite DM or email asking staff directly about ticket sales or timing.
  4. Consider attending a smaller monthly card first if crowds make you nervous; regulars are friendly and happy to explain unspoken rituals.
  5. If you want deeper stories (for blogging or social posts), talk with regulars outside during intermission, they know which wrestlers are local legends or which merch sells out quickest.

Your Next Step: Lock In Those Dates Early

The simple move that makes all the difference: Set digital reminders two months before both major EWF events. Don’t wait for word-of-mouth, check their official channels yourself and reach out early if you have questions about seating, access needs, or group tickets.

Every year someone gets left outside because they banked on luck instead of planning ahead. Make this the year you don’t miss out, and maybe start your own tradition along the way.
